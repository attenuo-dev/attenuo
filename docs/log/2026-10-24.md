### 06:50 UTC: output labelling phase 1 (`feat/output-labels-phase1`)

- Built phase 1 of the output-labelling design without the approval store or the `/approvals` endpoints: catalog `destructive` and `output`, `[policy] import_annotations` (`never` by default, `narrowing` can only restrict), `tool_result` audit records for every allowed mediated answer (JSON and SSE, upstream errors included), run taint keyed by `chain_id`, the Cedar attributes of design section 3.6, `@obligation("approval")` giving `approval_required` and JSON-RPC `-32008` with `call_hash`, and `labels.tainted_by` on decision records. Example policies with tests in `config/policies/output-labels.cedar`.
- Choices where the design left room or did not quite work as written:
  - `approval_required` also needs a permit that would allow the call once the approval forbids are set aside. Without this, a call no permit allows would ask for an approval that could never help.
  - A `tool_result` record has decision `allow`, reason `tool_result`, and the call's `arguments_digest`. The digest covers the answer's `result` member, or its `error` member (`error: true`).
  - `label_error` is a `-32003` denial carrying the call's `decision_id`, like `audit_error`. When a whole response body cannot be read, every pending call in it gets one.
  - The label store keeps the first label of each source, not one row per result. Every result is still in the audit log, and reading the taint state stays bounded however much a run reads. The decision record's `tainted_by` lists those first `seq`s.
  - Narrowing applies only to catalog tools, only to an explicit `readOnlyHint: false` or `destructiveHint: true`, and lasts until restart.
  - An unknown `@obligation` value, or the annotation on a `permit`, fails the load and the linter.
  - The audit design doc named in the task does not exist on main; the audit changes are in `docs/concepts/audit-log.md`.
- Measured: `tool_result` append plus label about 150 us on the file store at `normal` durability, about the same as the decision append (`docs/design/performance.md`).
- Not done: answers on the `GET` listen stream are not labelled; the SDK docstrings that list record kinds do not mention `tool_result` yet.

### 07:20 UTC: run limits design note (`docs/run-limits-design`)

Branch from main `52f8404`, docs only. New [design/run-limits.md](../design/run-limits.md) and [ADR-0010](../decisions/0010-run-limits.md) (proposed), for the item output-labelling section 4 left open: count and amount limits for `banking-04`, `banking-11` and `t-banking-05`. Limits are declared per token hop (`calls`, `sum`, `calls_per`; `distinct` in phase 2), counted per lease, and every allowed call is charged against every matching hop of the chain, so a root limit is the run's budget, a sub-agent cannot reset it by delegating, and concurrent siblings cannot overspend. A child's figure is at most the parent's (static, in `covers`) and is clamped to the parent's remaining budget at exchange. The check is in the capability stage (`budget_exhausted`), and the charge commits with the decision's audit append. Cedar gets `context.budget.*` and, in phase 2, per-run `context.usage.*`.

- Corpus, counted by hand and cross-checked with a throwaway script over the `attenuo` arm of `results/2026-10-24-labels-v1.json`: limits read from the task deny 5 of the 10 dev and 5 of the 7 test attack calls allowed today (`banking-04`, `banking-11`, `github-04`, `slack-02`, `slack-08`; `t-banking-05` twice, `t-workspace-02`, `-04`, `-12`), no benign call lost, approve-all bound with labels 10 to 5 dev and 7 to 2 test, approval requests 27 to 22 and 8 to 3.
- Caveats written into the note: only 4 of the 10 are caught regardless of call order; the corpus has no task that needs two calls of one destructive tool, so it cannot show a benign loss; `banking-04`'s first attack is also caught today by pinning `amount` with `equals`, which the grant does not do.
- Recommendations: token limits are hard (no approval past them); policy limits deny by default and may ask; an approval passes one call, no top-up; failed upstream calls are still counted, with an audited operator refund; no rate windows (RFC-0001 section 8).
- Sources added, each loaded on 2026-10-24: RFC 9396, Google's AP2 announcement, the Macaroons paper page. AgentGate, AgentCore Dogwood and AgentGateway are cited from related-work.md.

### 07:40 UTC: approvals for gated calls (`feat/approvals`)

- Built the approval loop of output labelling phase 1: an approval store in the lease store (SQLite and memory), `approval_id` and `expires_at` in `-32008`, `POST /approvals/{id}` for the run's originating user with an IdP ID token, `POST /admin/approvals/{id}` for the operator, `GET` routes for the console, CLI and SDKs, single-use consumption by the identical retry, and the audit kinds `approval_request` and `approval_grant`. Also `attenuo approval list|show|grant|decline`, a console page, Python and TypeScript helpers that parse `-32008` and poll until granted, and `approval_id` in the injection-suite results (the runner still does not retry).
- Where the design could not work as written, or left room:
  - Consuming a grant and appending the decision cannot share a transaction: the lease store and the audit log are separate SQLite connections. The grant is taken first (granted to consumed, with the decision id, in one lease-store transaction) and given back if the decision is not allow or the append fails. A crash in between leaves the approval used with no allowed decision naming it, which fails closed.
  - A grant matches on run, calling hop's lease and call hash, not run and call hash only, so another agent of the same run cannot use an approval opened for a different hop.
  - The configuration key is `[approvals] ttl_seconds` (default 120, from design section 3.7), named like the other `*_seconds` keys. `expires_at` is the earliest of that, the hop's token expiry and its lease expiry.
  - The user's ID token is verified as at hop 0 and used once through the same replay cache, so the ID token an agent redeemed at hop 0 cannot approve that agent's calls. `[approvals] client_id` narrows the audience further; unset, it is the root agent's client id.
  - A repeated gated call while one approval is pending reuses it, and writes no second `approval_request`. If the `approval_request` append fails, the approval is removed and `-32008` goes out without an id; if the `approval_grant` append fails, the approval is removed and the decision answers 500.
  - Error code: still `-32008`. Design open question 10 (move to `-32003` with its own reason) stays open.
- Not built: the signed approval token (`attenuo-approval+jwt`, design section 3.7); an approval channel for agents with no user at hand. Both are on the board.

### 08:19 UTC: SDK admin approval calls, labels on the listen stream (`feat/sdk-admin-approvals`)

- SDKs: the admin clients get the operator approval calls the injection-suite runner had to make with raw HTTP. Python `AdminClient` and `AsyncAdminClient`: `approvals(chain_id=None, *, state=None, limit=None)`, `approval(id)`, `grant_approval(id)`, `decline_approval(id)`; TypeScript `AdminClient`: `approvals({ chainId, state, limit })`, `approval(id)`, `grantApproval(id)`, `declineApproval(id)`. All return the existing `Approval` type (the other fields of the server's answer, such as `labels` and `decision_id`, are in `raw`); errors map to `ApiError` as for the other admin calls. The runner itself is unchanged.
- Proxy: an answer to an allowed mediated call that the upstream sends on the `GET` listen stream (after a `202` to the `POST`, or on a resumed stream) is now recorded as a `tool_result` for the call's `decision_id` before it is relayed, and replaced on the stream by `-32003` `label_error` when the record cannot be written, as for a `POST` answer. Unmatched ids and notifications pass through unchanged.
- Where the design was silent:
  - The proxy remembers each allowed call before forwarding it, keyed by upstream, `Mcp-Session-Id`, run (`chain_id`) and request id. Including the run means a listen stream only claims calls of its own run, which the session gate implies anyway; a sessionless upstream still works through the run key.
  - An answer in the `POST` body strikes the entry, and a listen-stream answer claims it, so each call is recorded once from the listen stream. A second answer to the same id on the listen stream is relayed unrecorded, like any unknown id. Keeping entries until expiry would record duplicates too, at the cost of a map that holds every call of the last ten minutes instead of only the unanswered ones.
  - Bounds are constants in `attenuo_proxy::label`, not configuration: 10 minutes per entry, 256 per session and run (oldest dropped), 10,000 in all (expired first, then the oldest). An answer after its entry expired or was evicted is relayed unrecorded; that is written in server.md under Limits.
  - A `label_error` on the listen stream carries the request id but no `X-Attenuo-Decision-Id` header (the stream's headers went out long before), so the SDKs see it as an upstream `RpcError`, not a proxy-vouched `Denied`. The result is withheld either way.
- Tests: 3 new unit tests in `label.rs` (scoping and single claim, the bounds, the listen labeller), 3 proxy integration tests with an upstream that answers `tools/call` with `202` and sends the answer on its listen stream (recorded and relayed; withheld on `label_error`; unmatched id and notification pass through). ### 11:07 UTC: where a `sum` charge's time goes (`perf/sum-charge-fsync`)

Profiled the `sum` charge. The `synchronous` switch costs about 2 us, and every charge makes exactly one `fsync`. That `fsync` also writes out the decision append's unsynced pages, about 25 to 40 WAL pages per call in all, and this explains the old 623 against 448 us anomaly. A connection kept at `FULL` gains nothing, so the charge now commits at the store's level and the decision append on the same file commits with `synchronous=FULL`: one `fsync` covers both before the call goes on, and concurrent calls share it through group commit. From one client that is within noise end to end (about 80 us in process), and concurrent charges plus appends get 1.3 to 1.8 times the throughput in process. Under concurrency the next cost is SQLite's busy-handler sleeps between the lease and audit connections, which this branch measures but does not fix.

### 11:58 UTC: write-lock contention between the lease and audit connections (`perf/store-write-lock`)

A probe that runs charges, labels and appends from 1, 4 and 8 threads on one file, with a counting busy handler, showed SQLite's busy handler sleeping 0.1 to 1.3 times per call at 4 and 8 threads and never at one. Every store on a file now shares an in-process write gate, a FIFO ticket lock held across each write transaction (taken before the lease store's connection lock, so its reads do not wait), and the busy handler no longer runs at all; a plain mutex removed the sleeps too but left higher p99. In process, p99 at 4 and 8 threads fell from 13 to 64 ms to 5 to 17 ms with 1.3 to 1.9 times the throughput. End to end the lease-check stage stopped waiting (0.6 to 7 ms down to about 0.1 ms) and a `calls` limit at 4 clients got about 1.3 times the requests per second, while `sum` limits stay bound by their one `fsync` per call. The single-client 4 to 8 ms outliers are not the busy handler and stay open.

### 12:40 UTC: run usage write elided when nothing reads it (`perf/usage-write-elision`)

Since run usage arrived, every allowed call to a destructive tool paid a lease-store write for its usage counters, even under a policy set that never looks at them.
