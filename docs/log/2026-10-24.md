### 00:13 UTC: Listen-stream endings counted, `stream.end` records bounded per chain and reason (`feat/proxy-stream-endings`)

Item (3) of the proxy follow-ups (left by `feat/proxy-followups-2`): dropped listen streams were log lines only, and `token_expired` `stream.end` volume.
- Premise checked first. A client that reopens its listen stream with an expired token is refused at authentication (`401`, `token_expired`) and was never recorded: `token_expired` is not a lease refusal, and only `LeaseInactive` after full verification is recorded (SR-18). So the `token_expired` volume is concurrent listen streams of one token (several sessions, or more than one stream) all cut at the same expiry, one record each; the same holds for a revocation. Reopening on a revoked lease is a different record, one `http.GET` `deny` per request, and stays unbounded (board).
- Metric `proxy_streams_ended_total{cause}`, fixed set `token_expired`, `lease_revoked`, `lease_expired`, `lease_unknown`, `lease_error`, `lease_other` (the guard's recorded reasons, `of_lease_reason`), `recheck_unavailable`, `recheck_failed` (the re-check's unrecorded failures), `client_disconnect`, `upstream_eof`, `upstream_error` (`StreamEndCause`). Counted in `Drop for State`, which runs exactly once whatever ended the stream (a body never polled included); the cut and upstream paths set the cause before returning, a missing cause means the client went away. An upstream error chunk now marks the stream done (it ended the body anyway), so it is not miscounted as a disconnect. Only the `GET` listen stream is counted (`StreamGuard::listen`); streamed `POST` answers are not. Reported through a new `Authorizer::observe_stream_end` (no-op default), as `observe_stage` is. No new records for disconnects or upstream ends; the four log lines gained `chain_id`.
- Bound: `StreamEndRecords`, shared by the router's listen streams, keyed by (chain digest, reason), window `STREAM_END_RECORD_WINDOW` = 60 s (a constant: no `[proxy]` or `[audit]` key fits a knob on record suppression, and the count is in the metric). `admit` opens the window under the lock, so of several concurrent ends exactly one records; the window runs from the first record, so a condition that lasts gets one record per minute and the first is never replaced. A suppressed end keeps its final event with `data.decision_id` naming the window's record (`None` in the microseconds before it is written), logs `record_suppressed = true`, and counts in `audit_records_suppressed_total{tool="stream.end",reason}` via `Authorizer::observe_record_suppressed`. The next record logs how many were suppressed before it. Decision records have no free-text field, so the count is in the metric and log only. Table capped at 10,000 keys; full of open windows, an end is recorded (fails towards more records). Digest rather than `chain_id` so a sub-agent's stream ends are not hidden behind its parent's record.

### 00:30 UTC: Run-state retention: counters, charge log and approvals of pruned runs (`feat/run-state-retention`)

The open note of the Leases item and of the #100 entry: `budget_counters`, `budget_charges` and `approvals` grew with every charged or gated call, forever, and #100 deliberately left them behind when it pruned a run's leases. (Written just after midnight UTC; appended here for the lead to roll over.)
- Rule. This state belongs to leases, and only ever goes after them. Nothing kept under a lease that is still in the registry is deleted, whatever its age: live, revoked or expired, a run's counters, charge log and approvals stay as long as its lease rows do, and those wait `[leases] retention_seconds` (a week) after the run ended. So a granted approval that has not been used yet, or a counter a live hop still charges, is never touched. Once the leases are gone (pruned by `prune_leases`, or never known to the store), a new `[leases] run_state_retention_seconds` applies: a charged decision's log entry goes once every lease it charged is unknown and its charge and any refund are older than the retention (decided per decision, since one decision's rows span several hops of one run); a counter goes once its lease is unknown and no charge under that lease is left in the log, so it outlives its newest charge by at least the retention (counters have no timestamp of their own); an approval goes once neither its lease nor its run's chain id is known and its creation, decision and expiry are all older than the retention (`run_state_prunable`, `approval_last_touched`). Approvals: an approval is never pruned before it expired; a pending or granted one of a gone run is pruned like the others, because it cannot be used any more (`take_approval` needs a live lease and the lease is unknown).
- Why per lease and not per run: the counters and charges are keyed by lease id and never joined with `leases` (ADR-0010); once the lease rows are gone there is no way to map a lease back to its run, and since `prune_leases` deletes whole runs, "this lease is unknown" means "its run is gone" (or the store never had it). Deleting a counter cannot reopen a budget: the hops it counted are unknown, so their calls are refused as `lease_unknown` before any limit is read.
- Default 2592000 (30 days), 0 off. The charge log is what an operator refund works from and what an incident review reads next to the audit log, and refunds follow billing or incident processes measured in weeks; a month on top of the week of lease retention covers a monthly cycle. A charge row is about 200 bytes, so a month of them is small next to the audit log. No startup warning: the rule already waits for the leases, so a short value cannot touch anything usable.
- What an operator loses for such a run: refund history (a refund of one of its decisions answers 404 `not_found`, exactly like a decision that charged nothing, so a late refund cannot be honoured and a duplicate cannot be told from a first one) and approval history (`GET /admin/approvals`, the console). The audit log keeps every decision record with its `budget` member, every `admin.budget.refund` record and every `approval_request` and `approval_grant` record.
- Refund of a pruned charge. Both stores already answered `UnknownCharge` for a decision with no log entry, and the route maps that to 404 `not_found`; nothing panicked or silently succeeded. The 404 detail now says the charge log may have been pruned (`[leases] run_state_retention_seconds`), and so does the `attenuo budget refund` line (still exit 2; the code stays `not_found`, which the CLI keys on). A decision that was refunded and then pruned answers 404, not 409.
- Store: `LeaseStore::prune_run_state(now, retention, max_rows) -> RunStatePrune {examined, pruned_counters, pruned_charges, pruned_approvals, wrapped}` on both stores (the lease store owns the three tables; one method, as asked). One cursor walks the charge log, then the counters, then the approvals, each in insertion order (SQLite `rowid`; memory map order), carrying over to the next table within a pass while the batch lasts, and reports `wrapped` after the approvals, so in one sweep the counters phase sees the charges just deleted. One `IMMEDIATE` transaction per pass under the write gate (memory: the rows lock held for the pass). `pruned_charges` counts decisions, not rows. Timestamps are parsed in Rust rather than compared as text, since RFC 3339 strings with and without fractional seconds do not sort. The memory store's charge log now records when a decision was charged and refunded (it ignored both before).
- Ticker: the third pass of `spawn_lease_maintenance`, after sessions and leases (no new timer), through `prune_in_passes`, whose counting callback now takes what a pass returned and gives back the total, so one pass can feed three series. The task starts when any of the three retentions is on. Metric `run_state_pruned_total{kind}` with the fixed set `counters`, `charges`, `approvals`, written at zero; the metrics.rs change is the new field, its method and its render block next to `leases_pruned_total`, nothing at the end of the file.
- Tests: `attenuo-audit` `crates/attenuo-audit/tests/run_state.rs`, both stores: `state_of_a_run_still_in_the_registry_is_kept_whatever_its_age` (live, then revoked, then a sweep a thousand weeks later: nothing goes, the refund still works), `state_of_a_pruned_run_is_kept_within_the_retention_and_deleted_past_it` (a never-registered lease's state goes past the retention; alice's run revoked and pruned: kept at exactly the retention, `d1` and the root's counter go past it while `c`'s counters stay with the refunded `d2`, the three approvals go past their expiry, then the rest; refunds of `d1` and `d2` are `UnknownCharge`; bob's live run keeps its counter, charge and granted approval), `a_granted_unconsumed_approval_of_a_live_run_is_kept` (a retention of one second, a sweep a thousand weeks later, then it is consumed), `a_refund_of_a_pruned_charge_is_refused_as_unknown`, `run_state_passes_are_bounded_and_resume` (40 decisions and 40 counters, passes of 7: twelve passes, never more than 7 rows, only the last short). ### 01:20 UTC: Lease-refusal records bounded per chain, tool and reason (`fix/refusal-record-bound`)

The open note of `feat/proxy-stream-endings` (board, proxy follow-ups item; and the `feat/audit-rejected-opens` item): a client that keeps retrying an undecided request (`initialize`, `tools/list`, notifications, `GET`, `DELETE`) on a revoked, expired or unknown lease added one `deny` record per HTTP request (`record_lease_refusal`), unbounded until its token expired. (Written just after midnight UTC on 2026-10-25; appended here for the lead to roll over.)

### 02:25 UTC: Lease prune passes bounded for wide runs (`fix/lease-prune-wide-runs`)

The open item of the Leases bullet and of the `feat/lease-retention` and `fix/proptest-and-maintenance-knobs` entries: a lease pass read and deleted the whole subtree of every prunable run in its window, in one transaction, so one very wide run made one pass as long as the run was wide. - Bound. The window query still reads up to `max_rows` rows at once; rows after the point where the budget ran out are read again by the next pass.
- Rule unchanged. A run (a top, meaning a lease with no parent or whose parent row is missing, and its whole subtree) goes whole with its subjects, bindings, session bindings, hop limits, run labels and span index, only once every lease in it is revoked or expired and the last ended longer ago than the retention (`tree_prunable`, `lease_end`). A late revocation restarts the grace period. The four existing prune tests pass unchanged.
- Design (`RunSweep`, `prune_pass`, `PruneOps` in `lease.rs`; one engine for both stores, each supplying a window, a children page, a row read and a one-lease delete inside its pass's transaction). A top whose own row is live costs its one window row, as before. A top that ended past the retention starts a sweep. (1) Walk: breadth-first from the top, children read a page at a time (`parent = ? AND rowid > ?` on `leases_parent_idx`, with an `EXISTS` probe per child so leaves need no query of their own). The first lease that is live or ended within the retention ends the sweep: the run is kept and nothing of it was deleted. (2) Delete: only once the whole run has been walked, in reverse breadth-first order, so children go before parents and the top goes last. A run that does not fit in what is left of a pass is kept in the store's prune state (in memory, beside the window cursor, under the connection lock) and continued first by the next pass; the window waits at its top until it is done. Memory held: the run's lease ids, twice (order and set), only while that run is being pruned.
- What a concurrent check sees mid-prune. Nothing in a pruned run is ever active, before, during or after: every lease in it has ended, and the top (the first lease of every chain in the run) is there until the last pass. While its rows are there, a chain check answers `lease_revoked` or `lease_expired` (the pipeline checks revoked, expired, unknown in that order), and `lease_unknown` once they are gone. A check never sees a lease without its parent row from pruning. The brief's "`lease_unknown` from the first batch on" is not what happens: the order above means a chain that still has a revoked hop answers `lease_revoked` even with its root gone, so marking or deleting the root first would not have given it either. Hiding the whole run at once would have needed every reader to filter rows. The test asserts the invariant that does hold: never live, never orphaned, the root there while anything is, and every lease either gone or still as it was.
- What a concurrent write sees. The pass transaction no longer covers the whole decision, so the store watches the run between passes. While it is being walked, a renewal or re-registration of a lease already seen, a revocation of one (`revoke`, and every lease `revoke_selected` changed), a child registered under one, or the top's missing parent registered again, abandons the walk. The run is then decided again from its top on a later sweep, as a renewal inside one transaction would have kept it. Writes to leases not walked yet are seen when the walk gets there. Once deleting has started, a renewal or re-registration of a lease of the run is refused as `UnknownLease` ("reads as pruned"), so no part of it becomes live again. A revocation goes through and changes nothing usable. A child registered under a lease being deleted becomes the top of its own run once its parent goes, like a child registered after its parent was pruned (the server never does this: an exchange needs a live parent). The hook costs a renewal and a registration one uncontended mutex. Defence in depth for a second writer of the same file that bypasses the hooks: before each delete the row is read again, and if it could be used again the sweep stops. ### 03:20 UTC: Streaming `tools/call` in both SDKs (`feat/sdk-streaming-call-tool`)

The SDK follow-ups "streaming responses in `call_tool`" and "streaming in `callTool`".
- API. Python `Client.call_tool_stream(token, server, tool, arguments, session_id, *, approval_token, max_event_bytes)` returns an iterator, and `AsyncClient.call_tool_stream` an async iterator (`async for`, no `await`). TypeScript `AttenuoClient.callToolStream(token, server, tool, args, { sessionId, approvalToken, maxEventBytes, signal })` returns an `AsyncIterable`. Each yields a `StreamEvent` (`message`, `method`, `last_event_id` / `lastEventId`, `decision_id` / `decisionId`, and `result` plus `is_final` / `isFinal` on the last one). I made it a separate method, not `stream=True`, because the return type differs (an iterator, not a `ToolResult`): a flag would need overloads on a `Literal` to keep mypy and tsc exact, and `call_tool` stays exactly as it was. Argument errors (no token, a bad `max_event_bytes`) are raised at the call; the request goes out on the first `next`.
- Errors. A JSON-RPC error answer goes through the same code as `call_tool` (`rpc_result` / `rpcMessageResult`, now able to take a message already parsed from a stream), so a denial is `Denied` / `DeniedError` with the header proof (SR-32), a `-32008` is `ApprovalRequired`, an upstream's `-32003` without the header is `RpcError`. The proxy's stream-end event (`id: null`, `-32001`, `data.reason`, optional `data.decision_id`; `guard::end_event_recorded`) raises `Denied` / `DeniedError` with that reason and decision id, so `except Denied` around a call covers a revocation mid-stream. A stream that ends without the response raises `AttenuoError`; so does an event over the bound.
- Parsing. An incremental parser per SDK (`attenuo/_sse.py`, `src/sse.ts`): LF, CRLF and CR (a CR at the end of a read waits for, or skips, the LF), `data:` lines joined with `\n`, comments and `event:`/`retry:` ignored, `id:` kept across events (ignored if it holds NUL) for `Last-Event-ID`, an opening BOM stripped, strict UTF-8, a final event without its blank line dropped (as the proxy drops it). Events are yielded as soon as they parse, so the progress before a cut or an oversized event is still delivered. The bound is 16 MiB by default (`DEFAULT_MAX_EVENT_BYTES`, the proxy's `max_upstream_body_bytes` default), counted over an event's lines and over an unfinished line; a plain body has the same bound. TypeScript keeps an unfinished line as a list of chunks and joins it once, so a long line in small reads is not quadratic.

Python sends with `httpx` `stream=True`, reads a 400/401 body so `NonceCache` can see `use_dpop_nonce`, retries once, and closes the response in a `finally` (early exit closes it when the generator is closed or collected); the client `timeout` is per read in httpx already. TypeScript: `Http.request` takes a `signal` that replaces the whole-request timeout, and `callToolStream` uses one `AbortController` for the caller's `signal` and an idle timer of `timeoutMs` restarted on every read, so a long stream is not cut at 30 s and a silent one is; leaving `for await` cancels the body. `Http.timeoutMs` is now public (read-only).

### 03:35 UTC: Upstream look-alike stream-cut events (`fix/proxy-stream-cut-lookalike`)

The board item from #115: an upstream could send a message shaped like the proxy's stream-end event (`id: null`, error `-32001`, `data.reason`, `data.decision_id`), and `call_tool_stream` / `callToolStream` would report the call as cut by the proxy, naming a decision id that is in no record. - What: `attenuo_proxy::guard::has_stream_end_shape` is the protected shape: a response (no `method` member) whose `id` is `null` or absent and whose `error.code` is `-32001`, compared by value (`-32001.0` matches, as it does in Python and JavaScript). That is what the SDKs read as a cut, without their `data.reason` test, so nothing they would take for a cut gets through. `disown_stream_end` rewrites each such message (single or in a batch) to `-32005` with `data.reason` `upstream_stream_end_shape` (`attenuo_proxy::REASON_UPSTREAM_STREAM_END_SHAPE`) and the upstream's whole error object under `data.upstream_error`; `id` stays `null`, and each rewrite is a `warn` line without the upstream's text. A unit test checks that the proxy's own `end_event_recorded` output has the shape, which ties the rule to the event; the cut path itself is unchanged.
- Why this rule: rewriting, not dropping, so the client still learns the upstream failed and what it said. `-32005` (`CODE_UPSTREAM`) already means "the upstream answered with something the proxy will not relay", and the SDKs surface it as `RpcError`. Nesting the original under `data.upstream_error` rather than keeping its message and `data` at the top keeps `lease_revoked` and the fake `decision_id` out of the places a client or a human reads as the proxy's word. Kept tight: a `-32001` that answers the request by its id is the upstream's own (the proxy's HTTP 401 bodies are its only other `-32001`, and they never pass through the relay), so it is left alone.
- Where: at the points that already parse upstream messages. Buffered JSON answers (`buffer_and_rewrite`, every `POST` with a mediated call or a list request) after the list filter and before labelling. ### 04:49 UTC: SDK stream-cut tightening (`fix/sdk-stream-cut-tightening`)

The SDK-side half of the look-alike item on the board (#115, #117): what `call_tool_stream` / `callToolStream` accept as a proxy cut.
- Rule. A message of the stream-end shape (a response with `id` null or absent and error code `-32001`) is a cut only when `data.reason` is one the proxy sends and `data.decision_id` is absent or a UUID string; then it raises `Denied` / `DeniedError` as before. Anything else of that shape raises `RpcError` with code `-32001` and the message's `data`, which still ends the call, but not as a decision.
- Reasons. Read from `guard.rs`: the event carries `Failed::reason`, not the `StreamEndCause` label. With the server's authorizer that is `token_expired`, `lease_revoked`, `lease_expired`, `lease_unknown`, `lease_error`, or `unauthorized` (a re-check that failed for a token reason other than expiry, `RecheckFailed`, not recorded, so no id). `lease_other`, `recheck_unavailable` and `recheck_failed` are metric labels and never appear in an event, so they are not in the list. A custom `Authorizer` that returns another `LeaseInactive` or `Unavailable` reason now gets `RpcError` `-32001` in the SDKs instead of `Denied`.
- Decision id. `end_event_recorded` writes `Uuid::to_string()` or leaves the member out (no recording guard, a not-recorded cause, or a suppressed listen-stream end whose first record has no id yet). The SDKs accept the hyphenated form in either case; an explicit `null` or an empty string is not a cut.
- Where. Python `_sse.py`: `_STREAM_END_REASONS`, `_UUID` and `_is_stream_end`, private like the rest of the module (the package exports no reason constants); `stream_end_error` now returns `Denied | RpcError | None`. ### 05:26 UTC: SDK stream deadline, SR-82 part 2 (`feat/sdk-stream-deadline`)
- What: SR-82 part (2) from [pass 7](../research/security-review-2026-10-25-pass7.md#sr-82-sdk-sse-readers-quadratic-rescans-in-python-and-no-idle-bound-on-keep-alive-only-streams-info-open). ### 11:07 UTC: where a `sum` charge's time goes (`perf/sum-charge-fsync`)

### 11:58 UTC: write-lock contention between the lease and audit connections (`perf/store-write-lock`)

### 13:45 UTC: budget refund and budgets in the lease tree, from the CLI (`feat/cli-refund-budget`)

### 21:32 UTC: Revoke by MCP session (`feat/revoke-by-session`)

The last open selector of the lease item. Until now `POST /admin/leases/revoke` answered `session` with 400 `unsupported_selector`, because a session id was bound to its chain only in the proxy's in-memory registry and the audit log carries only a hash tag of it.
- Binding: when the proxy binds a session id that a `POST` response assigned (normally the answer to `initialize`) and the request did not carry, it hands the id's digest to the new `Authorizer::record_session` (default: nothing). That happens after `adopt_session`, so a session the proxy refused (taken by another principal, registry full, SR-49 cap) has already lost its header and is not recorded. The request got there only with a verified token and DPoP proof, and a lease check came first for `initialize` (SR-18: no write without full verification). The proxy edit is these few lines in `handle_post` plus the hook; `adopt_session` and the `GET`/`DELETE` path are untouched, to merge cleanly with the stream work going on at the same time.
- Hash: the brief said to store the id hashed the way the audit log tags it. The tag (`session_tag`) is the first 12 hex characters of SHA-256, 48 bits. Keyed on that, two sessions colliding would make a session revocation hit someone else's chain. So the store keys on the full SHA-256 (`session::session_digest`, new), and `session_tag` is now defined as its 12-character prefix: same hash function, an audit tag is still a prefix of the stored value, and no raw id is stored.
- Store: `LeaseStore::record_session(session, server, chain_id, now) -> bool` and `sessions(session)`, both stores; SQLite `lease_sessions (session, server, chain_id, bound_at, PRIMARY KEY (session, server))`, created at open like `lease_subjects`. Keyed with the server because ids are assigned by upstreams, and two upstreams can hand out the same id. An unknown `chain_id` is `UnknownLease`. The server writes the chain's first-hop lease (`chain_id_of`); failures are logged at `error` and the session stays usable (its chain can still be revoked by `chain_id`). The same object goes into the `admin.lease.revoke` arguments digest, so the record names the selector kind and the resolved chain. An unknown session behaves as an unknown chain id does today: a 200 with empty lists, `chain_ids: []`. The `warn` log line of a selector revocation now prints the shown selector rather than the internal enum, so the digest is not logged either.

### 23:10 UTC: Numeric `range` argument constraint; time ranges deferred with reasoning (`feat/range-constraint`)

The oldest open board item, RFC-0001 §3.1 "numeric and time-range constraints", split in two: numeric ranges built, time ranges deferred with a paragraph saying why.
- Wire form: `{"op": "range", "value": {"min": 0, "max": 2000}}`, inclusive, either bound (not both) optional and omitted when absent, `min <= max`, bounds JSON integers in `i64`. Deserialization is strict, by a hand-written visitor rather than a derived struct: no bound, an inverted range, a float or string bound, a member other than `min`/`max` (a misspelt `maximum` would otherwise read as "no upper bound"), a repeated member and a list (`[0, 5]`, which a derived struct accepts as a tuple) are all refused. That one path covers tokens, exchange requests (`invalid_request`), `--cap` and policy test files.
- Values: a range is satisfied by a Cedar `long` in the bounds, read as `to_cedar_json` reads numbers: an `i64`, or a whole float of magnitude at most 922337203685477 (`5.0` is `5`). Fractions, numeric strings, `null`, lists and integers beyond `i64` satisfy no range. `attenuo_core::as_long` mirrors the policy crate's `DECIMAL_MAX` rule; core cannot depend on the policy crate, so the constant is repeated with a comment.
- One constraint per argument, as before: a capability maps an argument name to one `ArgConstraint`, so `range` plus `one_of` on one argument cannot be written, and the docs send "1 to 100 but not 13" to Cedar.
- `⊑`: range in range by interval containment over `i64`, a missing bound being the end of the type, so it is exact (`max: 5` and `min: i64::MIN, max: 5` cover each other; a proptest checks `implies` against integer containment). `equals`/`one_of` in range when every member is an integer in it; range in `equals`/`one_of` only for a single-integer range (`[1, 3]` does not cover `one_of [1, 2, 3]`: sound, and the rule stays one line); `prefix` and `range` never relate. An inverted range, which only the unchecked enum variant can spell, is empty and implies everything vacuously, like an empty `one_of`.

### 23:58 UTC: Lease registry retention (`feat/lease-retention`)

The open note of the Leases item and of the #97 entry: the leases table, and with it `lease_subjects` and `lease_bindings`, grew by one row per issued token, forever.
- Rule. The unit of deletion is a run: a top lease (no parent, or a parent row the store no longer has) and its whole subtree. A run is deleted whole once every lease in it has ended longer ago than `[leases] retention_seconds`, where a lease ends when it was revoked, or when it expired if it never was (`lease_end`, `tree_prunable`). So every lease is revoked or expired and none can come back: a revoked lease is never renewed, and an expired one only while its token is valid, which the retention must outlast. One live lease anywhere in the run, or one that ended within the retention, keeps all of it, root included. Deleting whole runs is what keeps `ancestors_of`, `classify` and the revoke cascade honest: no surviving lease has a parent row that is gone, so the hot path never sees a known child under an unknown parent from pruning. A lease whose parent was already missing (a store that lost rows, or an exchange that registered a child after its parent was pruned, which needs a retention shorter than the exchange itself) is the top of its own run and is pruned on its own terms; its token was refused all along, since the chain names the missing parent.
- Revoked runs wait out the retention too, from the revocation. The brief allowed pruning them at once; nothing a caller can do changes either way, but waiting keeps the run in the console tree, in revoke-by-user previews and in `lease_revoked` (not `lease_unknown`) refusals for the retention, which is what an operator in an incident wants. Measuring from the revocation rather than the expiry matters for a lease a user-wide revocation reached after it expired: it was renewable until then (`a_late_revocation_restarts_the_grace_period`).
- After pruning, the run's token reads as unknown. `lease_unknown` is already a refusal reason and is audited unchanged: a `tools/call` is a `deny` record with that reason, a session request a 401 with a `deny` record, a renewal a 400 with a `deny` of `lease.renew` (all checked in the server test). Revoke by user, chain or session matches nothing, the console's lease tree answers 404, and the audit log keeps everything.
- Deleted with the leases, in the same transaction: `lease_subjects`, `lease_bindings`, `lease_sessions` (by `chain_id`), `hop_limits`, `run_labels` and `run_spans`, all keyed by the run's leases and useless without them. Not deleted: `budget_counters`, `budget_charges` and `approvals`. They grow with charged and gated calls rather than with tokens, and the charge log backs the operator refund, so pruning them is its own decision (board).
- Setting: `[leases] retention_seconds`, default 604800 (a week), 0 off. A week is far above the default token lifetime (`[issuer] default_ttl_seconds` 900) and the usual IdP credential lifetime that caps a root token, and leaves a week to look into an ended run in the console. A lease row with its subject and binding is a few hundred bytes. Too short a value fails closed (a pruned run's renewal is refused as `lease_unknown`), so it is a startup warning when below `default_ttl_seconds`, not an error.
- Store: `LeaseStore::prune_leases(now, retention, max_rows) -> LeasePrune {examined, pruned, wrapped}` on both stores. SQLite: one `IMMEDIATE` transaction per pass under the write gate, a `rowid` cursor over `leases` as in `prune_sessions`; each row in the window is classified as top or not by a `NOT EXISTS` on its parent, a top whose own row is still live is skipped without a tree walk, and the subtree of the others is read with the revocation's recursive query and deleted id by id. Memory: map order and an id cursor, the rows lock held for the pass. `examined` counts window rows; `pruned` counts deleted leases, descendants outside the window included.
- Ticker: one task, `spawn_lease_maintenance` (replacing `spawn_session_pruner`), every 60 s runs the session pass and then the lease pass, each through one `prune_in_passes` helper: passes of 1,000 rows on the blocking pool until one wraps or 100 have run. The constants are now `LEASE_MAINTENANCE_PERIOD`, `LEASE_PRUNE_BATCH`, `LEASE_PRUNE_MAX_PASSES`. Metric `leases_pruned_total`.
- Cost, measured once with a throwaway release-build test (not committed) on a file store of 100,000 leases (50,000 two-lease runs, half expired three weeks earlier): passes of 1,000 took 5.6 ms median, 29 ms max, while deleting; a pass over live rows only took 0.77 ms. Before the live-top skip a live-only pass took 9.5 ms, all of it subtree queries for runs that could not be pruned. A pass holds the lease store's connection, so a hot-path lease check that arrives during a deleting pass waits up to that long, once a minute and only while there is something to prune.
