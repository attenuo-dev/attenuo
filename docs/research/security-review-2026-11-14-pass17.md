## Findings

### SR-127: the authorization details claim splits a scopes limit at the ceiling (Medium, fixed)

- **Confirmed by reading and reproduced.** `IdJagCeiling::apply` forms the ceiling in steps, `meet(G, resource)`, then `meet(_, scope)`, then `meet(_, authorization_details)`, and `intake::narrow` checks splits (`attenuate_root`) twice: against the root grant and against the final ceiling. `meet(a, b)` keeps each capability of `b` attenuated against `a`, with `a`'s limits copied in (`Limits::clamp_to`), one capability of `b` at a time. So when the limit comes from the `scope` step and `authorization_details` names several narrower capabilities under it, the final ceiling holds one capability per named capability, each with the full figure. They are separate capabilities of the ceiling, so `attenuate_root` against the ceiling finds no two requested capabilities with one parent, and the root grant, which carries no limit here, finds none either. This is SR-121 again, one step earlier: the limit is copied into several capabilities by `meet` instead of by the request's attenuation.
- **Attacker.** The agent at hop 0, which asks its IdP for the `authorization_details` it wants (the draft lets the client ask), under an operator who put the budget in `scopes`.
- **Reproduced** through the whole server (a throwaway test in `tests/oidc.rs`, the harness of `an_id_jag_budget_is_not_split_across_capabilities_sr121`): root grant `banking:*`, `[idps.id_jag_ceiling] scopes = { payments = [banking:send_money with sum {amount, 100}] }`. An ID-JAG with `scope: "payments"` and nothing else: a request for `send_money` with `to` pinned to `alice` and one with `to` pinned to `bob` is refused with `limit_split_at_root`, as SR-121's fix intends. The same ID-JAG with `authorization_details` also naming those two capabilities (without limits): the same request is answered `200`, and the root token holds `send_money{to=alice}` and `send_money{to=bob}`, each with `sum` 100. The run can send 200, and one recipient per capability named, up to the 256 a claim may name.
- **Not affected.** A limit in the root grant: the request is attenuated against the grant first, and both requested capabilities take their limits from the grant's one capability, so the split is refused before the ceiling is read. A limit in `authorization_details` itself: it is the last step, and its capabilities are the ceiling's. `resource` carries no limits.
- **Severity.** Medium, as SR-121: a run budget the operator set is spent several times over by the party it limits, by the shape of what it asks for. It needs the operator to put limits in `scopes` (an option added in #326) and an IdP that signs `authorization_details` the client chose.
- **Options.** (1) In `narrow`, check splits against each step of the ceiling that has limited capabilities, not only the last: keep the intermediate sets that `apply` builds, and call `attenuate_root` of each over the result. Sound, small, and the same rule as SR-121, including its cost: it also refuses a split whose parts carry lower figures of their own, so an ID-JAG whose `authorization_details` names `alice` with 50 and `bob` with 50 under the scope's 100, answered `200` today and sound, would be refused. (2) Give each ceiling capability the index of the limited capability of the earlier step it took its limits from, and key the split check by that: the same answers as (1), one pass, but a new field through `meet`. (3) In `meet`, when several capabilities of `b` take their limits from one limited capability of `a`, keep none of them, so such a claim names nothing there: simple, but it turns the request into `id_jag_ceiling_empty` or `scope_exceeds_id_jag`, which says less than `limit_split_at_root`. (4) Document only. **Recommendation:** (1). Options (1) to (3) change the answer to requests that are answered `200` today, so this branch does (4): the configuration reference says, under `[idps.id_jag_ceiling]`, to keep a run budget out of `scopes` until this is closed, and the board has a row.
- **Test.** None committed, since nothing is fixed; the reproduction above was a throwaway test against the real server.
- **Fixed after the pass** (#336), with option 1. `IdJagCeiling::apply` keeps the ceiling as it stood before each step after the first when it holds a capability with limits (`Applied::limited_steps`: after `resource` when `scope` or `authorization_details` follows, after `scope` when `authorization_details` follows; the root grant is checked on its own and the final ceiling as before). `intake::narrow` makes the existing check against the root grant and the final ceiling first, so a request refused before keeps its answer, then calls `attenuate_root` of each kept step over the result, and refuses a split with the existing `400 invalid_scope` / `limit_split_at_root`, naming the two capabilities as requested and never the ceiling's limits. Option 2 was not taken: it would give the same answers through a new field carried by `meet`, where this reuses the one split rule. The answers that change, all under `[idps.id_jag_ceiling]` with a limit in a `scopes` capability (or a root grant limit that a step copied) and an ID-JAG carrying `authorization_details` after `scope` or `resource`: a request whose capabilities take their limits from two capabilities of the final ceiling that come from one limited capability of an earlier step, answered `200` before, is refused. Among them the reproduction above (`alice` and `bob` named by the claim without limits, two capabilities of 100 each before), and the sound case option 1 gives up: the claim naming `alice` with 50 and `bob` with 50 under the scope's 100, two capabilities of 50 before, now refused, as SR-121 refuses halves. What answers as before: the scope alone (refused against the final ceiling, as since SR-121); one recipient of those the claim names (one capability, `sum` 100); one capability with `one_of` [alice, bob] that the claim names (one `sum` 100); a limit only in `authorization_details` (each capability with its own figure, the last step); a ceiling with no limits (the split allowed); an ID-JAG with one claim; ID tokens; `POST /admin/root-token`; exchanges. SR-128's reproductions are not changed: they go through `POST /admin/root-token`, which this does not touch, and a kept step picks the parent of each capability by the same rule `attenuate_root` always used, so overlapping limited capabilities in a `scopes` entry are still not combined (the "100 in all, at most 30 to alice" shape in `scopes`, with `authorization_details` naming `alice` and `bob`, still gives 30 and 100). Tests: `a_scopes_budget_is_not_split_by_authorization_details_sr127` and `a_budget_in_authorization_details_is_kept_sr127` (`tests/oidc.rs`), `a_limited_step_of_the_ceiling_is_kept_for_the_split_check_sr127` (`idp/ceiling.rs`); the first fails before the fix at its first request (answered `200`, the token holding `send_money{to=alice}` and `send_money{to=bob}` with `sum` 100 each), and its case of halves also answered `200` before (checked with a throwaway run); the second passes before and after; the unit test reads the new field. The configuration reference states the rule in place of the known gap.

### SR-128: overlapping limited capabilities are not combined at hop 0 (Low, fixed)

- **Confirmed by reading and reproduced.** `attenuate_one` gives a requested capability the limits of one parent: the first capability of the grant, in canonical order, that covers it with its limits, else the first that covers its calls. When the entry holds two limited capabilities whose calls overlap, a root token capability under one of them is not charged by the other: the grant is not a hop, and `charges_for` charges only the token's own capabilities that permit the call. The run limits design says a limit on hop 0 is the run's budget for the calls it matches, and the configuration reference says a `[[root_grants]]` limit "is the run's budget because only one capability of the root token may take it". With overlapping limited capabilities, it is the budget of the one root token capability that took it, which SR-121's fix does not change: its rule is one root token capability per limited parent, and each of these comes from another parent.
- **Reproduced** with `POST /admin/root-token` through the whole server (a throwaway test in `tests/run_limits.rs`):
  - Root grant `send_money` with `recipient` in `["alice", "bob", "carol"]` and `sum` 100, and `send_money` with `recipient = "alice"` and `sum` 30 ("100 in all, at most 30 of it to alice"). The entry as it is, as the token (no capabilities requested): 30 to alice, then 71 to bob is refused with `budget_exhausted` (`used` 30 of 100). A request for `send_money{recipient=alice}` and `send_money{recipient in [bob, carol]}`: the first takes the alice capability as its parent (`equals` sorts before `one_of`), the second the wide one, so no parent is split, and the root token holds alice with 30 and bob or carol with 100. 30 to alice and 100 to bob are both allowed: 130.
  - Root grant `bank:*` with `calls` 10 and `bank:send_money` with `calls` 1 ("at most one transfer"). A request for `bank:send_money` alone takes `bank:*` as its parent (it sorts first, and neither covers a request with no limits, so the first that covers its calls is used): the root token holds `send_money` with `calls` 10, and three transfers in a row are allowed.
- **Attacker.** The agent at hop 0, which chooses its request; the precondition is an entry with overlapping limited capabilities (A8), or a ceiling whose claim holds them.
- **Severity.** Low. The root token stays within the entry read as a union of capabilities (each of its capabilities is covered by one capability of the entry), which is how attenuation has always read a grant, and in the second case the agent could equally ask for `bank:*` alone. What fails is the reading the documentation gives a limit at hop 0, for an entry shape it does not warn about. The excess is bounded by the overlapping figures, not multiplied by the number of capabilities requested as in SR-121.
- **Options.** (1) At hop 0, clamp each requested capability to the limits of every limited capability of the grant (or ceiling) that covers its calls, and apply the split rule to each of them: the first case is then refused (`limit_split_at_root`, both capabilities are within the wide one) and the second gets `calls` 1. It changes the token issued for requests that work today, and only containment is handled: two capabilities that only overlap (neither covers the other's calls) would need an intersection test the core does not have. (2) Refuse at start a `[[root_grants]]` entry with two limited capabilities one of which covers the other's calls: refuses configurations that start today, and does not reach a claim's capabilities. (3) Document: at hop 0 the limits of overlapping capabilities of one entry are not combined, so a tighter rule for some of the calls belongs in a policy over run usage, or in a capability that nothing wider in the entry covers. **Recommendation:** (3) now and (1) with SR-127's change, which touches the same check. This branch does (3): the configuration reference says so under `[[root_grants]]`, and the board has a row.
- **Test.** None committed; the reproductions were throwaway tests against the real server.
- **Fixed after the pass** (#341), with option 1. `CapabilitySet::attenuate_root` (core) gives each requested capability, in place of the limits of the one parent `attenuate` clamps it to, the limits of every limited capability of the grant (or ceiling, or kept step of it) that covers its calls, by the existing coverage relation (`Capability::covers_calls`), combined by `Limits::clamp_to_all`: `calls` is the lowest of the request's and theirs, and is set first; then each one's `sum`, `calls_per` and `distinct` is copied when the request has none of that kind and lowered to the lowest figure when it counts the same argument (and derivation), and a `calls_per` or `distinct` that the combined `calls` is at most is not copied, as at an exchange. One `Limits` holds one limit of each kind, so when two of them, or the request's own limit and one of them, set one kind on different arguments (`sum` over `amount` and `sum` over `fee`; `calls_per` on `to` and on `from` without a `calls` low enough to imply both; `distinct` on `url` and on `url#host`), the request is refused with a new error, `AttenuationError::LimitConflict`, `400 invalid_scope` / `limit_conflict_at_root`, naming the capability as requested and never the limits, rather than one limit dropped. The split rule is then applied to each of them: two result capabilities may not both fall under one limited capability (it covers the calls of both), whichever parent each took its limits from; that is `limit_split_at_root` as before. Hop-0 intake keeps the result of the check against each kept step of the ceiling (it was a check only), so a `scopes` limit that `meet` did not copy into the final ceiling is carried too. Exchanges are unchanged: they charge the parent hop. The answers that change, at `POST /admin/root-token` with `[[root_grants]]` and at hop-0 intake (against the root grant, the final ceiling and each kept step): a requested capability under a limited capability other than the one `attenuate` picks now carries its limits too, so the token differs (the second reproduction: `send_money` with `calls` 1, 10 before; `github:read_file` beside an unlimited `github:*` and a limited `github:read_file` takes the limit, none before); a request with two capabilities under one limited capability, answered `200` before because each took its limits from another capability, is refused with `limit_split_at_root` (the first reproduction: 30 and 100 before; two fetches under an unlimited `web:*` beside a limited `web:fetch`); and a request with one kind of limit on two arguments over one capability, answered `200` before with one of them, is refused with `limit_conflict_at_root`. Sound requests this gives up, as SR-121's rule does: lower figures that add up within the wide figure (alice with 30 and bob or carol with 70 under 100), and the entry written out as the request when one of its limited capabilities is within another, which is a split of the wider one; leaving out `capabilities` still mints the entry as it is, which every call charges whole. Still out of scope: a limited capability that only overlaps a requested capability (neither covers the other's calls) bounds nothing at hop 0, since the core has no intersection test; so a request for `bank:*` under the second entry gets `calls` 10 and its `send_money` calls are not bounded by the 1, as the severity note says the agent could always do. The configuration reference states the rule in place of the known gap. Tests: `overlapping_root_grant_limits_bound_one_capability_sr128`, `a_nested_root_grant_limit_is_not_skipped_sr128` and `root_grant_limits_one_capability_cannot_carry_are_refused_sr128` (`tests/run_limits.rs`), `overlapping_scopes_limits_are_combined_at_intake_sr128` and `limits_one_capability_cannot_carry_are_refused_sr128` (`tests/oidc.rs`), `every_limited_capability_over_a_request_bounds_it_sr128`, `covering_limits_combine_kind_by_kind_sr128` and `clamp_to_all_is_clamp_to_for_one_and_order_free_sr128` (core), the property `attenuate_root_is_attenuate_under_every_covering_limit`; the first two fail before the fix (`200` with 30 and 100; `calls` 10). Tests that pinned the old answer and now give the new one: `a_split_is_judged_by_the_parent_attenuate_picks_sr121` (two fetches under `web:*` and a limited `web:fetch`, and under a limited `web:fetch` beside an unlimited `web:fetch{url=a}`: allowed before, `LimitSplit` now) and `a_limited_step_of_the_ceiling_is_kept_for_the_split_check_sr127` (the "100 in all, at most 30 to alice" shape in `scopes`: the kept step passed and gave 30 and 100, `LimitSplit` now). The fuzz targets `capability_attenuate_root` and `idjag_ceiling` check the rule against a reference written from it.

### SR-129: a repeated claim value was expanded once per repeat (Low, fixed)

- **Confirmed by reading and reproduced.** `named_by_scope` split the `scope` claim on whitespace and, for every token, copied the capabilities `scopes` maps it to; `named_by_resource` did the same per element of a `resource` array. The copies were collected into a `Vec` before the set dropped the duplicates, so a claim that repeats one value cost time and memory in the number of repeats times the size of the mapping. Since #330 this runs on the blocking pool, which keeps it off the runtime workers but not off the process's memory.
- **Attacker.** The agent at hop 0, with an IdP that copies the requested scopes into the ID-JAG without removing repeats (or accepts a `resource` array as requested). The claim travels in the request body, which `POST /token` caps at 2 MiB.
- **Reproduced.** Unit level, debug build, the real `IdJagCeiling::apply`: a `scope` of 20,000 repeats of a token mapped to 64 capabilities (80 KB) took 2.6 s and raised the process's peak memory from 17 MB to 454 MB; with a token mapped to two capabilities, 1 MB of repeats took 228 ms and 5 MB took 1.3 s. The cost is linear, so a 2 MiB body with a 64-capability mapping would be about 25 times the first figure, some 11 GB.
- **Severity.** Low: denial of service, which is outside the threat model's security properties, and it needs an IdP that signs such a claim. Higher than Info because one request could exhaust the server's memory.
- **Fix.** Both functions collect the distinct values first (a `BTreeSet<&str>`), then expand each once. The ceiling is the same set as before, so no answer changes.
- **Test.** `a_repeated_claim_value_is_expanded_once_sr129` (`idp/ceiling.rs`) counts the capabilities copied out of the mappings (a test-only counter, not a timing): 64 for the token once and for 20,000 repeats, mixed whitespace and an unmapped token between them, and the same ceiling; one for a `resource` array of 20,000 equal identifiers. With the old collection put back for `scope`, it fails with 1,280,000.

### SR-130: a start-up refusal named a root grant's subject under `redact_subjects` (Info, fixed)

- **Confirmed by reading and reproduced.** #334 names a refused `[[root_grants]]` entry by index, issuer, agent and subject (`RootGrant::describe`). The subject of a per-user grant is a user's identifier, usually an email address. With `[logging] redact_subjects`, the operator asked that the server's log lines name users by a keyed digest only, and the refusal is written to the same output by whatever runs the server. A configuration with `redact_subjects = true` and a grant for `alice@example.com` whose limit has an empty `arg` was refused with `... subject "alice@example.com"): limit ...`.
- **Severity.** Info: the subject comes from the operator's own file, and only an invalid configuration is refused.
- **Fix.** Under `redact_subjects` the message says `a subject (not shown: [logging] redact_subjects)`; the index still names the entry. The digest is not used because the issuer key it is derived from is not loaded when the configuration is checked. Without the setting the message is unchanged. The configuration reference says so.
- **Test.** `root_grant_limits_no_token_may_carry_refuse_to_start` (`tests/hardening.rs`) gained the case: the message names entry 1 and does not contain `alice`. With `describe(false)` put back it fails.

### SR-131: a scopes limit over several limited grant capabilities bounded none of them (Low, fixed)

Found after the pass, on 2026-11-15, while fixing SR-128 (#341): the `idjag_ceiling` fuzz target's budget property for `scopes` could not be stated on every input, and this is why.

- **Confirmed by reading and reproduced.** `meet(G, named)` keeps a capability of `G` unchanged when a single capability the claim names covers it, with its own limits. With a root grant of `send_money` to alice with `sum` 50 and `send_money` to bob with `sum` 50, and `scopes = { payments = [send_money with sum 60] }`, an ID-JAG with `scope = "payments"` gives the ceiling {alice 50, bob 50}: each is within the scope's 60, so each is kept as it is. No step is recorded (the grant is checked on its own, and there is one claim), so the `scopes` capability with its 60 is in no set the hop-0 check looks at. A request for alice and for bob is answered `200` with 50 each: 100 against the scope's 60.
- **Reproduced** through the whole server by the committed test below, which fails on the code before the fix (it gets `200`).
- **Attacker.** The agent at hop 0, which chooses its request. The preconditions are the opt-in ceiling, a `scopes` (or `resources`, or `authorization_details`) capability with a limit, and a root grant with two or more limited capabilities under it whose figures are each within the claim's.
- **Severity.** Low. The root token stays within the root grant, limits included, so nothing the entry itself forbids is reached. What fails is the ceiling's extra bound, for one entry shape. SR-127 (Medium) reached the same excess with an unlimited grant, which is the common case; this needs a grant that already carries its own limits.
- **Fixed after the pass** ((this PR)). `IdJagCeiling::apply` records what each claim named when it holds limits (`Applied::limited_claims`), and intake runs `attenuate_root` of each over the narrowed request after the kept steps, keeping the result. The request above is refused with `limit_split_at_root` (alice and bob are both under the scope's capability); one recipient alone gets the grant's 50. The check is the strict one of SR-121 and SR-128, so it also refuses the two recipients when the scope's figure would hold both (`sum` 200): ask for one capability the grant covers, or give the grant one capability for both.
- **Tests.** `a_scopes_limit_over_several_grant_capabilities_is_one_budget_sr131` (`tests/oidc.rs`), `what_a_limited_claim_named_is_kept_for_the_split_check_sr131` (`idp/ceiling.rs`). The `idjag_ceiling` fuzz target now checks the `scopes` budget on every input.

## Tried and held

- **More than one copy of a limited capability through `attenuate_root`** (#328), by reading against the code and the SR-121 tests. A wildcard and an exact capability under one limited parent (`bank:*` and `bank:send_money` under `bank:*` with `calls`), two overlapping `one_of` lists, and a request asking lower figures of its own all take the same parent index, so they are refused; two requested capabilities that are equal after clamping (`send_money` with `sum` 100 asked for and `send_money` with nothing) are one capability of the result. Names are ASCII and compared byte for byte, so `Bank` and `bank` are two names, and a request for one is not covered by a capability for the other. A ceiling built under a limited grant capability cannot split it, since the request is attenuated against the grant before the ceiling is read (SR-127 is the case where the limit is not in the grant). `POST /admin/root-token` without `capabilities` issues the entry as it is, so its overlapping limits are charged together; with them, it runs `attenuate_root`. A renewal reads no grant. An exchange is not hop 0 and charges the parent hop.
- **The prepared and the plain implication** (#329). `value_key` writes numbers with `number_key`, the function `value_eq` compares, so `1`, `1.0`, `1e0` and `-0` agree, and `2^53 + 1` stays apart from the float `2^53`. The one way a number could be written as nothing, a non-finite float (`format_f64` gives `None`, `number_key` then the empty string, which would make `[x]` key as `[]`), cannot occur: `serde_json` is built with `float_roundtrip` and `preserve_order` only (`cargo tree -e features`), so a number out of range is refused at parsing. With `preserve_order` a map still holds each name once (the last value wins), so the key's sorted members and `value_eq`'s lookup agree on duplicate keys. Strings are compared as bytes on both paths (`é` in NFC and NFD apart, `é` and `é` parsed alike); a lone surrogate escape is refused by the parser. The hash set's hash is a per-process keyed SipHash and equality compares the bytes after the hash, so colliding values cost time, at most the 1,024 values of a bound, and never change an answer. The `one_of` summaries were checked against `path_within` and `starts_with` by reading (the path summary: every value of the list has the base's segments exactly when their longest common run does, and the `dir` rule reads the fewest segments of a value without a trailing `/`). The existing generated tests compare every answer with the pairwise code.
- **The request bounds at every entry point.** The exchange (`attenuate_with`), hop-0 intake (`attenuate_root` against the grant and the ceiling, `attenuate_each` only after the request passed them) and `POST /admin/root-token` (`attenuate_root`, or `DelegationChain::root` without a grant) check `validate_size` before comparing anything. AuthZEN and the proxy compare no capability sets: they verify a token, whose claims `jsonwebtoken::decode` parses only after the signature is checked, and call `permits`. `DelegationChain::validate_at` does not check sizes again, but every hop of a signed token was checked when it was minted. The ceiling itself is not checked against the bounds: it is at most the grant and the claim's capabilities, and with SR-129 fixed at most every configured `scopes` capability, which the operator writes.
- **An ID token where only ID-JAGs are accepted, or the reverse** (#330). The kind is the request's (`subject_token_type`, or an ID-JAG for the jwt-bearer grant), and the IdP-signed header `typ` must agree: an ID-JAG needs `oauth-id-jag+jwt` (with or without `application/`, case ignored), an ID token may carry no `…+jwt` type, so a missing `typ` is only ever an ID token. `credentials` is checked with that kind right after the issuer lookup, and the `aud` the signature then binds differs (this server's issuer for an ID-JAG, the agent's client id for an ID token). An agent cannot change `typ` without the IdP's key. Approval ID tokens skip `credentials`, as designed.
- **A queue on the blocking pool** (#330). tokio cannot cancel a blocking closure, so when a client drops an intake request whose narrowing has been handed off, the work runs to the end after its `max_in_flight` permit is released. Measured with a throwaway test, debug build, `max_in_flight = 1`, a grant of 1,024 capabilities and a request of 1,024 (a full intake took 440 ms, the attenuation against the grant 135 ms of it, measured alone): 16 requests in a row, each dropped by the client after 330 ms, raised the process's thread count from 7 to 9 at most, so no work piled up. The rest of a request (form decoding, the signature, reading the set, then issuance) takes longer than the narrowing, so a client that drops its requests keeps about one extra narrowing running per permit, not an unbounded queue. Not measured: a shape of grant and request where the narrowing is the larger part of the request. The exchange has had the same property since it moved there. `[limits] token_requests_per_minute`, off by default, bounds the rate per agent.
- **The sink's CA, hostname check and redirects** (#332). `sink_client` builds a client per sink, so the sink's CAs are trusted by nothing else (the key fetches build their own, per entry); the web PKI roots stay, and `the_hostname_check_stays` holds. Redirects are not followed.
- **The sink's error chain** (#332). `without_url()` strips the URL from reqwest's top-level error only, and `error_chain` then adds its sources. No source names the URL: a TLS failure with an unknown issuer and one with a certificate for another name, against a URL with markers in its user info, path and query, carry none of them. Committed as `a_tls_failure_does_not_carry_the_url` (`tests/sink_ca.rs`). The second error names the certificate's DNS names, which the receiver chose; rustls only holds valid DNS names there. `last_error` is logged and kept in the sink's status; `/readyz` reports `degraded`, not the text.
- **`s3_error_code_of` and a hostile store** (#332). At most 4,096 bytes are read, and reqwest's per-request timeout covers the body, so an endless body ends the attempt at `timeout_seconds`: a throwaway store answering `403` with a chunked body that never ends was reported as `S3 answered 403 Forbidden` after 2.0 s (the timeout), and the `<Code>` it sent, with an escape character in it, was not reported. A slow error body therefore makes a failed attempt last up to the timeout instead of ending at the status; the queue retries the same way either way. A code is reported only as 1 to 64 ASCII letters and digits.
- **Other values in start-up errors** (#333, #334). Besides the subject (SR-130), the refusals name the issuer, the agent, the capability as written and the limit, none of them secrets. `sink_roots` names the path and the I/O or PEM error, not the file's content.
- **The ceiling once more** (#326). A `scope` sent as an array, a `resource` that differs in case or by a trailing slash, and `authorization_details` naming `*:*` with a limit under `banking:*` all name nothing, so the request is refused. Overlapping limited capabilities in a claim behave as in SR-128.
